Five ways to get through tonight

Rebuild the book

The reservations are not gone. They sit in booking emails, the morning printout, voicemail, and on the guests' own phones. An agent reads all of it into one list, drops duplicates, and seats it against your tables. Tab: Rebuild.

Confirm every guest

One text per reservation. The agent reads replies in plain language, changes party size or time if a table allows, and offers the nearest open times if not. Silence becomes a call list for the host, largest party first. Tab: Confirm guests.

Know your own availability

Table list plus turn times is enough to compute every open slot locally. Double bookings and guests with no table show up before service instead of at the door. Tab: Tonight's book.

Keep taking bookings

A fallback booking link for your Instagram bio, Google profile, website banner and phone greeting. It writes into the same book the host is reading. Tab: Take a booking.

Sync back when Resy returns

Every change made during the outage is logged below, so one person can re-enter it and both books agree.

If the brief means a diner or concierge trying to reach restaurants: point the same agent outward. It phones each restaurant's line, asks about tonight for a given party size, and logs who has a table.

The first hour

  1. Minutes 0–5. Photograph any screen that still shows today's book. Pull the printed sheet. Search the inbox for today's booking emails.
  2. Minutes 5–15. Paste everything into Rebuild. Fix whatever lands in the red box.
  3. Minutes 15–30. Send the confirmation round. Post the fallback link and change the phone greeting.
  4. Minutes 30–60. Call the guests who did not reply, largest party first.
  5. Service. Hold two tables until 7:30 PM for guests you could not recover. Honor any Resy confirmation a guest shows on their phone.
  6. After. Re-enter the change log into Resy.

Sixty-second pitch

  1. Problem. At 1:42 PM the restaurant loses its only copy of tonight: who is coming, when, and which tables are free.
  2. Insight. The data is scattered, not lost. Inbox, paper, voicemail and the guests themselves each hold a piece.
  3. Demo. Paste the mess, get a seated book with the conflict flagged. Run the round, watch replies update tables. Take a new booking against live availability.
